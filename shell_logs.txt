  313  * {binary,binary2}-bal.rep
  314  The allocation pattern is to alternatively allocate a small-sized
  315  chunk of memory and a large-sized chunk. The small-sized chunks
  316  (either 16 or 64 ) are deliberately set to be power of 2 while the
  317  large-size chunks (either 112 or 448) are not a power of 2. Defeats
  318  buddy algorithms. However, a simple-minded algorithm might prevail in
  319  this scenario because a first-fit scheme will be good enough.
  320  * coalescing-bal.rep
  321  Repeatedly allocate two equal-sized chunks (4095 in size) and release
  322  them, and then immediately allocate and free a chunk twice as big
  323  (8190). This tests if the students' algorithm ever really releases
  324  memory and does coalescing. The size is chosen to give advantage to
  325  tree-based or segrated fits algorithms where there is no header or
  326  footer overhead.
  327  * {random,random2}-bal.rep
  328  Random allocate and free requesets that simply test the correctness
  329  and robustness of the algorithm.
  330  * {realloc,realloc2}-bal.rep
  331  Reallocate previously allocated blocks interleaved by other allocation
  332  request. The purpose is to test whether a certain amount of internal
  333  fragments are allocated or not. Naive realloc implementations that
  334  always realloc a brand new block will suffer.
  335  * hard testcase are designed to test you code under realistic programs. 
  336  These testcases are the tracefiles from the hardest testcases you saw in CI Lab.
